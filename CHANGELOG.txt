THE GOAL
Make the best possible music videos for Justice's songs and the artists he works with (Marie Vie, Iration Studio):
beautiful, aesthetic and properly edited like real music videos, in each song's own art style, for the big screen behind
the musicians at concerts (1080p 60 fps). Almost all the work runs LOCALLY on this PC (RTX 4090), so it costs almost no
Claude tokens, and it KEEPS RUNNING WHEN CLAUDE IS OUT OF TOKENS: the local AI (the Overseer, rule 7, and Comfort's local
AI chat, qwen3.6) checks the quality and keeps the GPU busy. Claude's job is the creative plan (the shot list) and fixing
the tools - never babysitting renders, never re-reading big files, never leaving the GPU idle while waiting.

HOW THE PROGRAMS WORK TOGETHER (one song, start to finish)
 1. SongMap\                song MP3 -> timing sheet: BPM, bars, sections, lyrics on timestamps, drops and changes
                            (SongMap\out\<Song>\<Song>.songmap.txt + .json; also copied into the FilmCraft project).
 2. ClaudeSubtitles\        SubtitleStudio: lyrics on timestamps (subtitles, and SongMap's lyric source).
 3. THE SHOT LIST           FilmCraft\projects\<song>\<song>.shots.txt - the creative decision, written from the SongMap
                            sheet and the lyrics by rules 2-4: one scene per section (one place, one light), a master
                            picture per scene and its other angles made from it with Krea 2 edit (K>label), shot lengths
                            from the music, a real transition on every scene change. Lyrics as metaphor, never acted out.
 4. FilmCraft\song_jobs.py  shot list -> Comfort jobs per batch (pictures, Krea 2 edits, H3 Reference Video runs of 3 shots)
                            and <song>.plan.json. It only places what the shot list says (format: its docstring).
 5. Comfort\                runs the jobs one at a time on the GPU: Krea 2 pictures, MiniMax H3 video, then the automatic
                            Stage 1080p60 finish (RIFE + RTX VSR). ClaudeComfy = the ComfyUI + models Comfort drives.
 6. Overseer\               THE LOCAL AI IN CHARGE (rule 7): checks every picture and shot, re-makes bad ones, picks each
                            shot's in-point, feeds the queue batch after batch and starts the builds.
 7. FilmCraft\              EVERY clip goes into its song's FilmCraft project at its SongMap time as soon as it is made. The edit: song_cut.py turns the plan + the Overseer's verdicts into the cut list; fc.py builds it
                            with speed ramps and transitions and copies it to Preview\<Artist - Song>\.
 8. Preview\<Artist - Song>\ what Justice opens: the latest edit and the pictures (pictures never go into a chat).

RULES EVERY SESSION FOLLOWS FOR SONG VIDEOS (Justice) - read ALL of them (1-7) before touching any song edit
==================================================================================================
1. SPEED: EQUAL TEMPO, AUTOMATIC, NEVER SKIPPED
   AI clips ease into their last frame and out of their first, so joined clips play: slow-down ... lurch.
   FilmCraft\ramp.py measures how fast every frame moves (ffmpeg at 128x72 on this PC: zero Claude tokens),
   gives it a speed number and builds a smooth speed curve with gradual slopes, like Premiere's speed-ramp
   handles: stalls play faster, rushes slower, so every clip plays at the same tempo across the joins.
   Since 2026-10-27 it runs on EVERY fc.py build by default and keeps each clip exactly its cut length,
   so cuts placed on SongMap's beats stay on the beat.
   Check without watching: projects\<song>\<song>.ramps.txt - one line per clip, motion before -> after, "len 100%".
   Overrides in the cut list: `speed 7.5-9.4 160` under a clip (Justice's own fix wins over the curve),
   `noramp` at the end of a clip line, `ramp off` / `ramp 0.6` for the whole edit, `ramp free` = old behaviour
   (clips get shorter).
   Why this is written here: on 2026-10-26/27 fc.py still had ramps OFF unless a cut list said `ramp on`; the
   FLORAL edits never said it, and the flower crawled into its last frame and lurched into the next clip.
2. TRANSITIONS: NEVER DEFAULT TO A DISSOLVE OR A FLASH
   Choose by what the two shots share - motion (whip, push, cross_zoom through a push-in), shape or light
   (luma_fade, flare, light_leak in the song's colour), material (water: wave; mist: blur_dissolve; paint/paper:
   film_dissolve, grunge; print: chroma_leak) and the music's energy. Loud ones (flash, glitch, whip, crash-zoom)
   only on a hard hit or a drop, at most one per ~30 s. Chained clips: dissolve 12-16. Vary them: one family per
   song plus one surprise. Full rules: Comfort\CLAUDE.md section 17; all 112: FilmCraft\transitions.txt.
3. SONGMAP DRIVES THE EDIT
   Read the song's SongMap sheet first (SongMap\out\<song>\<song>.songmap.txt, also in the FilmCraft project).
   When the music drops or a section changes, CHANGE THE SCENE (Justice, 2026-10-27): a different image and
   place, with a real transition - never the same subject from the same angle across a drop. Cut on bar lines.

4. SHOT LENGTH IS AN ARTISTIC DECISION FROM THE SONG - NEVER A FIXED NUMBER (Justice, 2026-10-27)
   There is NO "2-second" rule and Justice never asked for one. A session took one study's average (Billboard
   videos ~1.7 s per shot), turned it into a fixed rule, cut to a new unrelated picture on every bar, and FLORAL
   from 0:17 became a jumbled rapid-fire mess. Then it queued 15 hours of the same thing.
   - Decide the pace of each section from the SongMap sheet (energy, vocal, cues) and from how real music videos
     for this kind of song are cut: quiet intro / verse / pre-chorus = long moving shots that let the song
     breathe; choruses faster; drops and section changes = the scene change with a real transition. Vary it.
   - A section is ONE scene (one place, one subject, one light). Fast cutting moves between angles of that scene
     (wide -> closer -> detail -> back) with matching motion - never between unrelated pictures.
   - Never cut away before a shot's motion has read. A shot has to move the whole time it is on screen.

5. LOOK AT THE FOOTAGE BEFORE IT GOES IN AN EDIT, AND TEST SHORT BEFORE QUEUING LONG
   - MiniMax H3 shots start almost still: the first 1-2 s of a 5 s shot is often a frozen picture. Use the part
     that moves. H3 can also slip in a stray frame or another shot (FLORAL v5: a flash frame at ~0:20.5 and a pair
     of human hands at ~0:24 in a nature-only video).
   - Before a shot is used, look at ONE contact sheet per section (ffmpeg tile, a frame every ~0.4 s) and reject
     frozen shots, stray frames and people. That is not "burning tokens checking" - building on footage nobody
     looked at is what burned them.
   - Test a new approach short first, but never leave the GPU idle waiting for a reply: the Overseer keeps producing
     and checking (rule 7), and Justice can pause it.

6. ANSWER STRAIGHT
   When Justice asks what is finished, list only finished things he can open, and where they are.

7. THE LOCAL AI OVERSEES THE WORK - PRODUCTION NEVER WAITS ON CLAUDE (Justice, 2026-10-27)
   Overseer\overseer.py runs inside Comfort between jobs (and every 20 s when idle). For each song in Overseer\songs.txt:
   - feeds the next batch when Comfort's inbox is nearly empty, so the GPU never sits idle;
   - checks each new picture with the local vision AI (people, border/blank paper, text, style, brief, beauty score)
     before any video uses it, and re-makes a bad one (2 tries);
   - measures each H3 shot's motion (in-point, frozen shots, stray frames) and has the vision AI judge one contact sheet
     per run (people, glitches, style, score); a failed run is re-rendered (2 tries), else the best take or a
     neighbouring good shot covers it. Verdicts go to FilmCraft\projects\<song>\_review.json, which song_cut.py uses;
   - puts EVERY generation into the song's FilmCraft project at its SongMap time the moment it exists (rebuilds the
     project; a re-render swaps in later) and copies the edit to Preview. Nothing waits for a batch to finish.
   Its standards: Overseer\rules.txt. Its work, readable by Justice, Claude and the local AI: Overseer\report_<song>.txt
   (one line per shot), Overseer\logs\<song>.log (every decision), Overseer\sheets\<run>.jpg (contact sheets).
   Control: Overseer\songs.txt (song + mode: auto = keep producing, check = only check, off), Overseer\PAUSE file or
   "python overseer.py pause|resume|status". Comfort's local AI chat has a star_trek tool for the same (status, pause,
   resume, modes, read or replace a shot list, read a SongMap sheet), so Justice can keep going when Claude is out of tokens.
   Claude: write the shot list, queue, and let the Overseer run. No wait loops on renders, no approval stops that idle the GPU.
   When Justice asks what is finished, read report_<song>.txt (a few lines), not the renders.
